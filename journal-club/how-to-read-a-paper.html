How to read a paper

Pick one anesthesia paper and walk it in this order. The numbers stay on the page. You still have to make the call, then say it in one sentence.

Work one paper. Every step below follows the paper you pick.

Numbers are from each paper’s abstract. Validity notes that are not in the abstract match the session handout.

  1. Start from the decision

    Name the choice the paper might change before you read the abstract. If you cannot say what you would do differently, you do not have a question yet.

  2. Write the PICO

    Population, intervention, comparison, outcome, and when that outcome was measured. The same block or the same monitor can still be a different question.

  3. Name the design

    A randomized trial, a systematic review, and a practice review do not support a plan in the same way.

  4. Ask whether the results are valid

    Internal validity. One or two notes on each question is enough for the session.

    • Was randomization adequate?
    • Was allocation concealed?
    • Who was blinded (patients, clinicians, outcome assessors)?
    • Were baseline characteristics similar between groups?
    • Was intention-to-treat analysis performed?
    • Were crossovers or missing data substantial enough to affect the results?

  5. Read the size of the result

    Magnitude and precision. A p-value does not say whether you would change the plan.

    • What were the effect sizes (HR, OR, RR, ARR, NNT, or NNH)?
    • What were the confidence intervals, and do they include a difference you would act on?
    • Were the results clinically meaningful, or only statistically significant?
    • Were harms or adverse effects reported?
    • Were there time-course patterns, such as an early difference that later closed?

  6. Ask whether it applies to this patient

    Applicability. The patient in front of you is the last check.

    • Does the study population match my patients (age, comorbidities, setting)?
    • Are the outcomes patient-centered (survival, function, pain, satisfaction)?
    • Are there practical barriers here (staffing, resources, protocols)?
    • How might local pathways differ from the study?

  7. Write the BLUF

    One sentence: what you would do differently, or the same, because of this evidence. That sentence is what you give in the opening minutes of the session.
