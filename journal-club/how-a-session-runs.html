How a session runs

A session is a discussion, not a lecture. A BLUF is the bottom line up front: one sentence on what you would do differently, or the same, because of the evidence.

How to read a paper is the order for a single article before you walk in.

  1. 0–5 min

    Welcome, objectives, and housekeeping

    Faculty

  2. 5–15 min

    BLUF for each article, about a minute or two

    Resident leads

  3. 15–30 min

    Validity and results, walked through the checklist

    Group

  4. 30–45 min

    Applicability, using a clinical vignette

    Group

  5. 45–55 min

    Does this change our practice?

    Group

  6. 55–60 min

    Take-home points and anything to follow up

    Faculty

Preparing a paper

Start with the question, the design, and the result that would change a plan. Then ask whether the conclusion is supported, and whether it applies to the patient in front of you. The blank handout is the checklist used in each session.

Critical appraisal series

Seven parts from the resident appraisal curriculum, one per study design, each ending in the questions worth asking out loud. It is the curriculum behind the middle of this hour. It is not the facilitator script.

  1. Foundations, PICO, and the EQUATOR network

    Why appraisal ends in a decision, and how a vague question becomes a PICO with a reporting guideline beside it.

  2. Randomized trials and CONSORT

    Randomization, allocation concealment, blinding, and reading the size of an effect instead of stopping at a p-value.

  3. Observational studies and STROBE

    Cohort versus case-control, and confounding, selection, and measurement.

  4. Systematic reviews, PRISMA, and figure forensics

    How a review is assembled, and how to read a forest plot without taking the summary estimate on faith.

  5. GRADE

    Moving from one study to a judgment about certainty, one outcome at a time.

  6. AI-assisted research

    What still has to be checked when a model helps search, screen, or draft.

  7. Machine-learning studies

    Appraising a prediction model: the data, the test set, calibration, and the difference between performance and benefit.
