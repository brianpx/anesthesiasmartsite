Critical appraisal series

Seven parts from the resident appraisal curriculum. Each part covers one design, the reporting guideline that goes with it, and the questions worth asking out loud at the table.

This is the curriculum. How a session runs is the hour itself. How to read a paper is the order to work a single article in before you walk in.

  1. Foundations, PICO, and the EQUATOR network

    Why appraisal ends in a decision, how a vague question becomes a PICO, and what a reporting guideline is for.

  2. Randomized trials and CONSORT

    Randomization, allocation concealment, blinding, intention to treat, and reading the size of an effect instead of stopping at a p-value.

  3. Observational studies and STROBE

    Cohort versus case-control, and the three problems that will not go away: confounding, selection, and measurement.

  4. Systematic reviews, PRISMA, and figure forensics

    How a review is assembled, and how to read a forest plot without taking the summary estimate on faith.

  5. GRADE

    Moving from one study to a judgment about certainty, one outcome at a time.

  6. AI-assisted research

    What a model can do for a search, a screen, or a draft, and what still has to be checked by hand.

  7. Machine-learning studies

    Appraising a prediction model: the data, the test set, calibration, and the difference between performance and benefit.

How to use it

  • Before your first session, read Part 1 and Part 2. Together they cover most of what a discussion turns on.
  • When you are assigned a paper, read the part that matches its design. The design is on the session page next to the citation.
  • Read Part 5 when you are preparing to answer the last question of the hour, which is whether this changes our practice.
  • Every part ends with an At the table checklist. Those are the questions to bring, not a script to read from.

What this is not

It is not the facilitator script, and it is not a substitute for reading the paper. It also does not treat a reporting guideline as a grade: CONSORT, STROBE, and PRISMA describe what belongs on the page, while RoB 2, ROBINS-I, and AMSTAR 2 are the tools for judging the study itself. That distinction is the first thing Part 1 sets up, and the rest of the series leans on it.
